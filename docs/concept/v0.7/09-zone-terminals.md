# Zone terminals

A zone is the group of fittings served by one local node. There are now two kinds of node, and the player carries the key to one of them.

**The loft computer.** The first program is written at the loft desk, on a cream and teal computer with a recessed glass screen, physical keys and a small connection lamp. `print()` displays words on its screen; Test also ghost-types the returned words beside the player. Send message delivers the greeting to Hotaru’s addressed-message bell. A different name still displays, without waking her. It needs nothing carried.

**Reach.** Code works on the world only from beside what it works on, as if the tablet were a short-range remote link. A zone’s fittings answer only while the traveller stands at that zone’s screen, having jacked into it (`FieldAccess.zone_in_reach`). A single fitting that names a `stand` cell answers while the traveller carries its required item and stands within a cell of that place (`FieldAccess.lamp_in_reach`): the stair lantern’s code is written at the stair lantern, and the whole round at the screen. The same distance, about one cell from an authored approach point, governs F for everything in these rooms; farther away nothing is offered. Field Notes (E) anywhere else is the plain open page. A later skill could relax this, so the rule lives in one place, `core/field_access.gd`. A returned command is checked against the traveller’s position at Apply, so walking off a screen drops the proposal.

**Nell’s field tablet.** After the greeting Hotaru sends the traveller to the quilt, where Nell left a slate with a cord and a brass plug. The tablet is a carried item (`core/player_items.gd`): it stays in the saved state through Undo, Reset and both rooms, and only an authored pickup can hand it over. It jacks into an area node, and it is what lets the traveller work on a single fitting from beside it. Anything wider than a fitting is not built. **Only a zone is plugged in** (user decision, 2026-10-30): at a zone's screen the slate is wired, its cord running to the socket; beside a single fitting it pairs over the air, like a short-range wireless link. The [interface notes](../../../pipeline/UI.md#the-glass-and-the-ghost-sparks--machines) describe how each looks.

**The workshop wall screen.** The workshop’s zone node is a wall-mounted glass panel on the west wall with a brass-collared socket under it, not a computer. It names its zone (`WORKSHOP`, or `AWAITING SLATE` before a link) and, once the tablet is plugged in, shows light counts and test output on the glass. Without the tablet it only describes its socket. Connect once; the tablet remembers that room’s link, and Field Notes work from anywhere in it. Room links stay distinct from zone connections.

Implemented: the shared terminal model in the loft, the wall screen downstairs, separate saved connections, range-checked interaction, clear connected-zone headers, live screen output, the carried-item rule and the existing preview/apply rules. Rootward Square now adds the first outdoor service pedestal, with the same physical slate/socket/range rule and three lamps. In a connected area with no ticket, its saved scratch page allows experimentation without inventing quest progress. Larger networks, fittings other than lamps and holding the tablet to something in reach beyond lamps remain future work.

## Authoring

An area document’s `terminals` list names the placed object, zone, safe approach, id and title. A lamp may name `stand` (the cell to stand on), `title` and `requires`; a quest may name `access` (`{"kind": "device", "id": lamp}` or `{"kind": "zone", "id": zone}`) so the job book, the ticket and the Field Notes pages know where it is written. Terminal optional fields: `requires` (an item id the traveller must carry; the model must still be a registered terminal), `prompt` (the full interaction label), `idle` (the words on the glass before a link) and `locked` (what the node says to a traveller without the item). A declared terminal zone’s fittings enter Python’s snapshot only after visitation and connection, and `ZoneTerminals.connect_at` refuses a terminal whose required item is not carried. The core validator enforces the same boundary for returned commands. Connections are discoveries, retained through Undo and Reset; source edits, movement, new connections and travel invalidate stale program proposals.

A top-level `pickups` list names an item (`PlayerItems.KNOWN`), the placed object that shows it, a title and the approach cell. The app hides the object once the item is carried and offers the pickup only after the story has sent the traveller to it (for the tablet, after Hotaru’s waking conversation).
