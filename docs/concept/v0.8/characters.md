# Characters — private history, public voice

Working character guide · 2026-11-02 · Companion to the [opening](storyboard.md) and [outdoor quest](outdoor-quest.md).

The user wants recurring story characters to have distinct histories, speech, wants and fears. The established identities and approved art remain the starting point. Biographical details below are **writer proposals**, not events the game has already established. They should shape choices and behavior before becoming exposition. Do not add every past event to someone's first conversation.

## How to use a profile

Give a recurring character a previous way of life, a present responsibility, something they want today, something they avoid admitting, and a relationship that makes those things visible. Then choose sentence rhythm and vocabulary. An accent without those things only changes the spelling of the same voice.

Regional accents can be suggested lightly through cadence and a small set of ordinary words. The fictional geography is not yet mapped to real-world accents. Avoid pages of phonetic spelling or assigning dialect by appearance. No voice should rely on a catchphrase in every exchange. Personal history is not a requirement that everyone has a secret tragedy.

**Influences are story shapes, not borrowed characters:** a bustling neighbourhood comedy for Braum; a late-career craft story for Iona; a restrained family/workshop story for Nell; travel notebooks and procedural investigations for Mira; working-waterfront stories for Rowan; family-garden chronicles for Tessa; a companion travel story for Hotaru. Mix their pressures and relationships into this town instead of transplanting a famous character's life, jokes or accent.

## Braum — makes things for people

**Existing identity:** carpenter/builder, dark curls, rust and burgundy work clothes, leather apron; house with the freshly painted fence. Story display name **Braum**, reusing existing `bram` / `sparks_bram` resources.

**Proposed past:** learned his trade making boarding ramps, crates and repairs on the waterfront. Came back uphill when his mother's knees made their old stairs difficult; rebuilding her entrance turned into work for half the street. He now knows who needs a wider gate or an extra handrail. His own fence is the one job he can repaint without negotiating a customer.

**Now:** wants the tram landing open and Iona's commission finished. Volunteers before checking how much time he has. His pride is in things people actually use, not elegant plans.

**Private pressure:** hates being the cheerful man everyone likes but nobody trusts to finish. It makes him overpromise. He can admit a gap in his knowledge once someone starts helping; he is not a permanently incompetent handyman.

**Voice:** broad local harbour cadence, energetic clauses, occasional “reckon” or “mind”. Starts with a confident answer, then adds a concrete qualification. Offers to carry/fetch/hold something. His joke tends to be on himself; he does not mock a beginner for needing help.

**Relationships:** Nell makes him slow down and check. Iona refuses an optimistic completion date but trusts his joinery. He wants the player to become a known neighbour, so learns their name instead of forever saying “Nell's apprentice”.

**Use sparingly:** “Done by lunch.” Only if the scene actually tests that promise.

**Sample:** “The frame's finished. Proper finished, this time. I need someone for what goes on the glass.”

**On a fault:** gets the tool roll and checks his mounting before blaming the new routine.

## Iona Venn — signs are for the person reading them

**New story character:** signwriter, approximately mid-forties. Cropped tight dark curls with a silver forelock, blue apron over a narrow work smock, paint on her cuffs; a different silhouette and palette from the existing women. A new shared-frame skin and portrait are needed.

**Proposed past:** apprenticed at a small commercial print shop, then painted shopfronts and boat names on her own. A long-standing customer closed last winter. The tram commission is her first changeable powered sign. She chose the glass face after the painted stop board needed replacing, rather than having the machine imposed on a helpless old craftsperson.

**Now:** wants a sign passengers can read while carrying bags and looking for the steps. Knows materials, contrast and layout. Needs Nell's help with the card, and can say so without pretending to be a programmer.

**Private pressure:** worries that her judgement will be treated as obsolete along with the old boards. She would rather learn the unfamiliar part than be told “the machine does it all”. This appears in what she insists on keeping, not a speech about changing times.

**Voice:** composed trade-register, short questions, measured answers. More open vowels and fewer dropped words than Braum; no eye-dialect. Talks about people reading, sight lines, weather and delivery. Unlike Mira, she is comfortable with “a little longer” when that describes a reader's experience. She is not a second mathematical perfectionist.

**Relationships:** argues with Braum about deadlines but accepts his practical help. Nell is a colleague whose expertise she hires. The player becomes someone she can ask a real question, not a child she showers with praise.

**Sample:** “Stand on the bottom step. If you have to stop walking to read it, I've made it too small.”

**On a fault:** shields the glass from glare and asks what the preview showed. She does not order a new lamp before checking the card.

## Nell — care expressed as preparation

**Established:** experienced automaton-maker, landlady, Hotaru's maker; absent on calls during the opening; bread, desk, slate and work ticket left for the lodger. Keep her approved face and selected clothes.

**Proposed past:** ran a busy repair shop with several people at different times. Apprentices and regular customers have moved on; she still lays out a spare stool. Knows the older town fittings because she has worked on many of them, not because she knows every answer to the mystery.

**Now:** wants today's calls cleared and someone else able to keep a routine working without waiting for her. Giving the player a small checked job is deliberate delegation.

**Private pressure:** finds it easier to leave a tool prepared than to ask someone to stay. Reluctant to admit the calls have become repetitive. Do not canonize illness, inability to write code, guilt over a disaster or a final Rootworks revelation just to explain why a novice gets the first job; those older concept possibilities remain open.

**Voice:** understated, practical, few modifiers. Sometimes one-word answers; important information often comes after a chore. Her occasional regional vocabulary is older workshop usage, not a comedy accent. Avoid making all her lines cryptic wisdom.

**Relationships:** Hotaru remembers the routines of her working days. Braum gets a fond, exacting check. Iona is another professional. She shows trust in the player with access and responsibility.

**Sample:** “The fitting's checked. Leave it on when you go. I'll have both hands full.”

**On a fault:** separates the observed symptom from the last repair and asks what changed.

## Hotaru — remembers the work, wants a place in it

**Established:** small rotor-driven robot, recently dozing while Nell is out. Counts and notices ordinary details. Companion, not a tutorial menu in disguise.

**Proposed history:** spent years nearby while Nell worked, reading labels aloud and remembering small preferences. Remembers those repeated moments more readily than dates. Nothing here establishes that she has been asleep for decades or secretly contains the explanation of the roots.

**Now:** wants to take part again, and to understand what the new lodger will do differently. Can have a preference, make an observation or choose to accompany someone without becoming an autonomous solution machine.

**Private pressure:** dislikes being left on a shelf when there is work she might do. Express this by offering a specific useful action. Do not turn every scene into self-pity or a reassurance exchange.

**Voice:** clear, literal, occasionally formal; simple unexpected observations. Uses a number when she actually counted. Unlike Rowan's compressed diagnosis, she often follows an observation with a sincere question. Unlike a telegram robot, she speaks naturally rather than printing capitals and STOP.

**Relationships:** has an old shared rhythm with Nell; learns a new one with the player. Braum's approximations puzzle her. Iona's reader tests give her something concrete to check.

**Sample:** “It's shown me the same welcome three times. Am I meant to go away between them?”

**On a fault:** reports the exact last thing that happened. The notebook gives the Python explanation.

## Mira — precision because places affect people

**Established:** surveyor/map-maker, brisk movement, measurements and shortcuts; existing cast skin and portrait.

**Proposed past:** travelled with a small surveying crew before taking the town maps. Once recorded the right address on the wrong side of a steep street; a delivery crew hauled equipment up the wrong flight. The correction is mundane and memorable, not a secret catastrophe.

**Now:** wants routes and records that agree with the actual street. In the power investigation she notices repeated addresses and branch patterns, not an abstract need to explain variables.

**Private pressure:** dislikes “close enough” most when it will inconvenience someone else. Can accept a rounded measurement when the distinction genuinely does not matter. She must be able to revise her own conclusion.

**Voice:** brisk, self-correcting, specific; asks “which one?” before offering an answer. Her travel experience gives a slightly less local vocabulary. No perpetual interruption gimmick and no lecture on programming metaphors.

**Relationships:** Braum's rough estimates irritate her, but she uses the handrails he built. Rowan checks her interpretations against the supply. The player can bring observations she did not have.

**Sample:** “Which stop? The platform says Spindle, the stair says Hill Street. No wonder people keep asking.”

**On a fault:** marks where and when it happened, then compares a second report.

## Rowan — a diagnosis needs evidence

**Established:** older sparkworks keeper/millwright, quiet and dry, existing cast art.

**Proposed past:** worked machinery around boats and wet yards before settling at the sparkworks. Has spent enough early shifts with unreliable equipment to distrust confident guesses. Remembers coworkers by habits and repairs more often than telling full anecdotes.

**Now:** wants a stable supply and a maintenance plan someone can actually carry out. Protects useful equipment from being replaced merely because it is old.

**Private pressure:** fears losing the habit of checking because he knows the machinery too well. His reserve is not lack of care; he shows up and brings the spare part.

**Voice:** short, unhurried, conditional. Silence can be a pause to inspect, not a mysterious ellipsis on every page. Waterfront vocabulary is concrete, used only where relevant. An occasional full warm sentence should be noticeable.

**Relationships:** respects Nell's work without agreeing with every diagnosis. Mira's records help him find what a gauge alone cannot. Braum is allowed to be enthusiastic after the load is supported.

**Sample:** “Glass is lit. Card's next. Don't take the frame down.”

**On a fault:** tests one thing and reports it. Never instantly knows the unseen root cause.

## Tessa — attentive beneath the digressions

**Established:** gardener/orchard keeper, warm, conversational, names trees; existing cast art.

**Proposed past:** helped move a family garden when their previous plot was built over. Cuttings survived with names and notes attached. Knows people through what grows where, who waters while someone is away and who always wants the first fruit.

**Now:** wants planting and watering to keep working while there are too many seasonal jobs for one pair of hands. She can run a garden; she is not merely a distracted person waiting for a coder.

**Private pressure:** keeps too many tasks herself because delegating once meant losing something she had kept alive. Her growth is allowing a reliable helper, with clear instructions, to do a share.

**Voice:** flowing clauses, a personal example, then a very direct request. Softer local cadence than Braum. Her tangents contain a relevant observation; not every tree joke needs a relative's name.

**Relationships:** works with Trowel as a colleague, feeds scraps into the neighbourhood's social network, notices the effects of outages before a ledger does. Can tell Nell she has taken too much on because she does it herself.

**Sample:** “Put the stop name first. My sister gets off with two bags and looks up for half a second. After that she's watching her feet.”

**On a fault:** notices the affected time or row of plants and offers a practical workaround while the cause is checked.

## The player — space for an ordinary person

Keep their earlier home, family and reason for arriving open unless the player later chooses them. Current personality comes from actions and short responses: curious, teasing or direct. Do not make every option the same question with a different punctuation mark.

They can admit “I've only done the lights”, set a boundary with “Later”, ask for help and make a joke without becoming a coward, an automatic employee or a programming prodigy. Routine dialogue choices need not themselves be code. Their growing local relationships, including Braum learning their name, are enough to establish a life here.

## Small roles need a small distinct thing

| Character | Want / habit | Useful line or behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Pip | Wants deliveries to have somewhere to go; apologizes to objects he has put in the wrong compartment | Prints a short acknowledgement, then corrects his own count. No new satchel; parcels stay in his established compartments. |
| Wick | Wants a defensible timetable; notices exceptions and writes them down | “LAMP THREE CHECKED. EARLY. AGAIN. STOP.” Precision is professional fuss, distinct from Hotaru's curiosity. |
| Trowel | Wants a row finished before beginning another | Turns back to straighten one pot. Little tape; does not become a full gardener lecturer. |
| Heron | Wants a load and the space beneath it clear | Very slow, exact acknowledgements. No comic unsafe swinging load over people. |
| Tender | Wants a reading she can confirm, checks before adjusting | Repeats a measurement in tape, with a changed number if it really changed. Does not reveal the root mystery by accident. |
| Tally | Wants a balanced exchange; insists both parties count | Exact small-money statements without teaching the player a shop economy that is not built. |
| Tram conductor, provisional side role | Wants boarding to flow; reads a sign from the passenger's point of view | “Give that page a moment. They've only just come off the step.” A name/backstory can wait until the conductor has a recurring role. |
| Anonymous passenger | Has a destination and a current inconvenience | Looking for a turn while carrying two bags; one observation is enough. No biography required for each crowd silhouette. |

Robots other than Hotaru keep their established short printed-tape voice. These proposed habits do not claim the existing ambient movement actually carries parcels, money or irrigation data.

## A quick voice check

The prompt is “Can you help with this sign?” The answer should remain identifiable with the name hidden:

- **Braum:** “I'll hold it. Or fetch someone. What d'you need first?”
- **Iona:** “Let me see it from the steps. No, leave the words up a moment.”
- **Nell:** “Socket's under the rim. Braum's left it open for you.”
- **Hotaru:** “Shall I read it out? I can stand where the passengers stand.”
- **Mira:** “Which side do they approach from? The map's got the old stair.”
- **Rowan:** “Show me what it's doing.”
- **Tessa:** “After I set this down. Don't leave without me; I use that stop.”

If every response ends with a polished joke, an aphorism about instructions or the same encouraging reassurance, rewrite some as plain work. Give other people things to do while the protagonist thinks. A serious or tender beat can be a held door, a corrected label or an offered tool; it does not need a backstory monologue.

## Before committing a recurring-character scene

Read the lines without speaker labels. Check that each person wants something present, acts from knowledge they could have, and can be wrong in a way related to their history. Show one biographical detail through an object or action before explaining it. Keep choice responses specific. Give a side character a short profile only when their role grows. The profiles are guides for scenes, not information cards the player must read before taking a job.
