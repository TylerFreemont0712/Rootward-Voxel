# Interface kit and screens

The interface is code-drawn from one palette, so it needs no texture files, scales cleanly and stays editable. Its look is *semi-fantasy*: dark warm wood and leather, thin brass trim, parchment text, engraved capitals. It follows the voxel concept's own HUD (dark brown plaques with gold borders) rather than ornate high fantasy. Playing needs no art service for any of it.

## Design rules

- **The world comes first.** Location stays above a translucent quest/next-step panel on the left; quest completion temporarily uses that same space. Code opens in a stitched notebook drawer on the right, with no scrim. Both journeys share the drawer and tracker, and the camera shifts to keep the player in the free area. Menus that take over the screen still use modals.
- **It is a learning game.** Every error says which line, what the mistake probably is and what name was likely meant; the raw Python message stays visible. Lessons, hints and a searchable reference live beside the editor, and nothing is real until Apply.
- **Honest about what exists.** The character screen shows the boy apprentice, with no equipment slots. The other skins belong to townspeople, never to the player. Unvisited places are fogged on the map.
- **Keyboard and mouse both work.** Menu buttons take focus (Tab/arrows, Enter); HUD buttons are mouse-only with hotkeys; Esc peels back one layer at a time.

## Where it lives

`ui/` is grouped by job: `kit/` (palette, themes, drawn style boxes, buttons, icons, modal and drawer bases), `hud/`
(the play overlay, job ticket, day dial, in-world prompts), `transition/` (the clockwork veil between places),
`story/` (dialogue window and log, in-world speech, the opening card, Nell's note), `notebook/` (the jobs journal,
job ledger, the loft's greeting lesson, field notes), `code/` (the editor, hover cards, name lookup, Dark+ palette,
execution playback, the slate), `build/` (build mode), `screens/` (title, pause, settings, character, map, property,
room finishes) and `dev/` (development panel, asset showcase and request queue). Fonts are in `ui/fonts/`. See
[NAMING](../docs/NAMING.md) for the project-wide rules.

## Scene change veil

Every change of place goes through `Game._fade` and `ui/transition/gear_veil.gd`. A toothed brass edge covers the doorway, seven brass/copper wheels turn over an engraved workshop dial, then the veil opens on the arrival. The wheel train is cached vector geometry (`clockwork_gears.gd`, `clockwork_wheel.gd`, `gear_geometry.gd`); the shader draws only the curtain, grain and its lip. All wheels use the same module, 20° involute flanks, pitch-circle spacing, a little backlash and opposite rotation in their tooth-count ratios. Followers derive their phase from their parent rather than independent clocks. Bolts, turned hubs, a luminous code core, moving conduit sparks and a labelled **City power** meter give it detail without rebuilding the wheel polygons each frame.

- `Game._fade(action, down_seconds, during_down, during_up, origin, up_seconds, lead_in)` awaits the action under full cover. Closing takes 0.55 seconds; opening normally takes 0.7. Full cover lasts at least 0.9 seconds, including the build. The new world gets eight rendered warm-up frames and another 0.25 seconds before revealing. Longer arrival walks extend the reveal while retaining the usual walking pace.
- Natural Sparks room travel uses `WorldView.setup_async`: threaded resource preparation, bounded outline calculations and placement batches yield real scene-tree frames. Newly placed models render progressively under the opaque curtain to warm GPU resources. The outgoing traveller's template/atlas remain referenced through the swap, avoiding a large synchronous texture reload at the end. Nodes are created on the main thread; the content is inactive while partially built. Ordinary synchronous setup remains available to tools and older areas, with `BuildPump` compatibility redraws.
- `begin_travel_walk` / `end_travel_walk` temporarily own the player's presentation motion. Disabled gameplay input cannot reset it to idle. Travel changes animation frames while interpolating through the doorway and stops at the actual endpoint; logical cells and collision remain authoritative.
- The gear clock advances only while visible and caps a missed frame's phase step, avoiding a large tooth jump after an OS/driver stall. It does not claim loading progress from elapsed time.
- `GearVeil.set_world_state` accepts an optional `city_power` snapshot: `strength` and `stability` in 0–1, `restored_links` in 0–3. These affect speed, source illumination, the meter and conduit lights. Missing data uses the steady neutral appearance. The snapshot is copied/clamped and never mutates progression. No power quest, programming API or saved city-power system is implemented yet. **Dev · F8 → Loading screen** previews Low power, Steady and Restored without changing the journey.

`test/test_transitions.gd` checks solid wheel profiles and non-overlap over a full tooth engagement, angular ratios, bounded/non-mutating power state, identical synchronous/incremental outlines, readable dialogue and actual keyboard-triggered travel with changing walk frames. `tools/reviews/dialogue_transition_review.gd` captures dialogue, power presets, motion and real crossings at 1600×1000 and 1280×720, writing per-phase cadence to `cadence.json`. [Current review](reviews/2026-11-02-dialogue-and-clockwork-transitions.md). Earlier curtain captures remain in [the original review](reviews/2026-10-30-doorways-transitions-and-flicker.md).

## The glass and the ghost (Sparks & Machines)

Nell's words stay on paper; code is on glass. `ui/code/tablet_screen.gd` (`TabletScreen`) is the code surface as an object: a bezel carrying a link lamp, its words and a close button along the top and keys along the bottom, and between them the glass (a file tab row, `content`, an OUTPUT strip and a status bar). Two looks share it: the loft **desk terminal** (cream casing, a row of keys) on the first greeting, where it sits below that page's paper; and Nell's **field slate** for every job page once the slate is carried. The slate is a painted frame (`assets/ui/nells-field-slate/`, built by `scripts/art.sh` from the [v0.73 concept](../docs/concept/v0.73/README.md)): cream enamel with a teal rim, brass corner guards, a jack and lamp in its top-left corner and a ledge below the glass. It is drawn as a nine-slice whose corners, jack and lamp never stretch; the glass fills its painted window, the link words and close button sit in a dark title bar at the top of the glass, the painted lamp glows when linked (and sends arcs when paired), the cord is seated in the painted jack, and the Test and Apply keys stand on the ledge below its groove. If the frame has not been built, the slate falls back to the drawn dark-enamel bezel.

**The field slate and the notepad.** With the slate, a job page is two things on screen at once, beside the room (`JobsJournal`). This is the general layout for code in the game. On the right the slate itself fills the drawer (about 47% of the width): a file tab per reached page, a resizable, collapsible sidebar with **FILES**, **DEVICES** (each lamp the link reaches: green when lit, a blue ring and `off → on` where a debug run proposes a change) and **VARIABLES** (the program's own names and values as a debugger shows them, following the replay step by step, then as they stood when the program finished; a list opens with ▸ to show its items with their indexes, and a name goes to where the program sets it), the editor and replay, the output and the **Debug** (a play-and-bug icon, F5 or Ctrl+Enter) and **Apply routine** keys on the bezel. While a debug run is showing, the status bar turns orange as a code editor's does; when a run would finish the job, OUTPUT says so in green and Apply glows; once Apply finishes the job, the keys give way to one green-lit **Job done · put the slate away** key that closes the slate and lets the story go on. Beside the slate's lower left is **Field Notes**, a spiral-bound notepad (`ui/notebook/field_notepad.gd`, `FieldNotepad`): a wire coil through punched holes down its left edge over a cardboard spine, ruled lines and a red margin, sheets peeking out underneath, and sticky markers standing up from its top edge for **Index** (the lessons reached so far, numbered from /00, each with its idea, done or open, and a way to it; it grows as lessons are reached), **Job** (Nell's words and the worked example), **Names** (the addresses on the link and the names the program makes up, with the last value a Test saw) and **Hints** (one more each time). A pencilled line under every page carries the Debug and Apply guidance. The notes are a guide, not a requirement: **Put away** tucks them down to their stickies (and gives the room their space), any sticky brings them back, and the choice holds for the rest of the session. The room draws back a little (`Game.SLATE_PULL_BACK`) and is framed left of both (`extra_reserve()`).

**Programs read like programs.** A job's starter is a small real script: a file-name header comment, named variables (`workshop`, `lamps`, the lamps on the card), and a `# TODO:` comment over the lines to fix. The editor marks a TODO comment and the lines under it, up to the next blank line, with a soft amber band and a gutter bar (`CodePalette.todo_lines`), so the area to change is obvious without leaving the program's real shape. The replay writes what each line set at the end of that line (`lamps = [Lamp('landing-lamp'), …]`) from the sandbox's variable evidence ([limits and evidence](../docs/PROGRAMMING.md#limits)).

**Reading a program as an editor does.** Hovering a name in the code shows a card (`ui/code/code_hover_card.gd`) with what it is and its signature, a line of explanation from the API reference, what it holds and where it is set: over `workshop.lamps`, *attribute · zone.lamps -> list[Lamp]*, "A list of the lamps in this zone", then the three lamps with their state. Ctrl+click on a variable goes to where it is set and selects it; Ctrl+click on an API name pins its card. `ui/code/code_lookup.gd` (`CodeLookup`) answers from the program text, `ApiReference` and a picture of the link (each visited zone's lamps and their state), preferring what the last debug run saw; it never runs the program.

**Wired or paired.** Zone screens and sign sockets are plugged in. At a zone's screen the slate's lamp reads `JACKED IN · zone · n devices`, its cord hangs from its edge, and in the room a cord runs from the traveller to the socket under the screen. Beside a single fitting the slate pairs over the air: its lamp sends out signal arcs (`PAIRED · fitting`) and small sparks hop from the traveller to the fitting (`scenes/slate_link.gd`, `SlateLink`). Sign pages use the same cord at their authored `SlateSocket` and direct print to that display. Reach is decided by `FieldAccess` / `DisplayCards`.

- **Colours** live in `ui/code/code_palette.gd` (`CodePalette`): VS Code Dark+ (`#1e1e1e` glass, blue definitions and `True`/`False`, purple control flow, yellow functions, orange strings, light-blue variables, green comments). `CodePalette.style(code)` dresses a `CodeEdit`; it also colours the names a program makes up (`lamp`, `lamps`, `world`) as variables. `CodePalette.screen_theme()` is the dark theme for the inputs, small buttons and labels on the glass. `CodePalette.snippet(text)` is a read-only example. `CodeEditor` keeps its own palette for the archived journeys.
- **Replay looks like a debugger.** `ProgramPlayback.use_screen_look()` marks the executing line with VS Code's yellow highlight and a gutter arrow, and writes the line's small values dim at its end. Without that call the older journeys keep the teal highlight.
- **A proposal is blue, a fact is warm.** `ui/kit/ghost_box.gd` (`GhostBox`) frames a Test's speech: translucent ice-blue glass, a dashed outline whose dashes slowly march (so it reads without colour), a breathing glow, thought-dots instead of a tail, a `TEST · GHOST` caption, a gentle bob and letters that fade in as they are typed (`WorldSpeech`). Real speech stays solid paper with a tail. World proposals use `art/ghost.gdshader`: a pulsing blue fill with a fresnel rim, and outlined edges on boxes (coral for what a program would take away). The tokens are `PaperPal.GHOST`, `GHOST_DEEP`, `GHOST_FILL` and `GHOST_INK`.
- **No controls hint.** The Sparks HUD no longer draws the lower-right controls card; the buttons carry tooltips. The shared `Hud` null-guards `_controls` so the archived journeys are unchanged.
- Reviews: [tablet screen and ghost text](reviews/2026-10-30-tablet-screen-and-ghost-text.md), [field slate and notepad](reviews/2026-10-30-field-slate-and-notepad.md); `tools/reviews/tablet_review.gd` captures the pages (run it at 1920×1080).

## Adding or changing a screen

**The main Sparks programming workspace is `JobsJournal`.** Fitting, zone and sign jobs reuse its field slate, notepad, file tabs, device/variable inspection, Dark+ code editor, execution replay and Debug/Apply controls. New puzzles should supply content and a validated connection context to this workspace. The first greeting retains its introductory teaching page on the same tablet component; ordinary later jobs use the shared workspace. The former standalone sign modal has been removed.

The field slate asks for 47% of the canvas width, bounded at 740–980 logical pixels. Its sidebar defaults to 196 pixels: drag the divider between 128 and 360, fold Files/Devices/Variables individually, or use the header sidebar icon / Ctrl+B. The shortcut also works with code focus. Layout preferences follow jobs/rooms for the play session. The notepad retains its clipped position, tabs and Put away behavior. The sign's card readout/page control sits beside the common debugger/output; its explicit output context is cleared when the slate closes. Editing a finished job restores Debug and Apply. [Native review](reviews/2026-11-02-tram-stop-and-shared-slate.md).

1. Build it from `UI.*` factories and theme variations. Use `Pal` colors and the existing glyphs; add a glyph to `ui/kit/glyph.gd` if you need one.
2. Make it a `Modal` if it takes over the screen. Give it a `setup(...)`, open it through `Game._push_modal`, and let Esc close it.
3. Add a shot to `tools/reviews/sparks_tour.gd` and run `scripts/screenshot.sh`. Look at it at 1600×1000 and at `--resolution 1280x720`.
4. Test behavior (not pixels) in `test/test_app.gd` or `test/test_ui.gd`: open it through the real path, assert what a player would see change, close it.

## Notebook editor behavior

Starter and Clear replace the program as one undoable edit. Text size, completion and bracket pairing are settings.
